# v0.19.1 — A prompt you can work

Light cycle plan under [[0.) Development Protocol 1.0]]
Opened 2026-08-29 · unplanned: opened *after* [[v0.19.0 — Practice items, the pool]] tagged,
because using the pool for real is what found these.

Addresses **Item pool**

> A chapter yields practice items of both kinds, grounded in its own prose, that I would
> actually sit down and answer — judged by reading them, and by an item surviving my
> editing untouched through a regeneration

## Problem

v0.19.0 shipped a pool that passed every exit criterion it set itself, and then the first
person to actually sit down with it hit three things no criterion covered.

**The items were not workable.** Chapter 1's first problem read *"Write the symbolic
logical formula that captures the meaning of the quoted sentence"* — and carried no quoted
sentence. Three of the 64 items pointed at material they never included ("the quoted
sentence", "the original sentence"). The v0.19.0 furniture filter could not see them: it
looks for references to the book's *apparatus* ("this section", "Table 2-1"), and these
name no book noun at all. The effect is worse than a furniture reference, which is merely
annoying — a prompt with a hole where its subject should be cannot be worked at all, and
self-grading has nothing to grade.

**The session was a flashcard deck.** One card at a time, no way back, no way to see the
paper. That is right for a twenty-second concept card and wrong for a ten-minute
derivation: you want to skim what is coming, skip one and return, and know how much is
left. v0.19.0 reused the review panel's shape without asking whether the shape fit.

**The text was chrome-sized.** Prompts rendered at 15px in the fleet's dense admin scale,
in a mostly empty card. This is a surface you sit and *read* at, and the Practice tab was
already rendering the same items through the reader's own renderer — so the session was
also the lookalike-drift the material-editor rule exists to prevent.

The through-line: **v0.19.0's exit criteria tested the mechanism, not the artifact.** Every
one of them passed while the thing on screen was unusable, because they asked "did
generation run, does regeneration spare edits, does fluency stay honest" and never "would
you answer this question".

## Appetite

One sitting. This is a repair of the surface v0.19.0 shipped, not new capability — if it
overruns, the navigator is the part to shed, because unworkable prompts are a correctness
bug and a cramped session is only unpleasant.

## Plan

1. **Self-containment.** The authoring prompt demands the item carry every input it acts
   on; `_DEIXIS_TELLS` drops pointer phrases as the deterministic backstop, same shape as
   the furniture gate.
2. **The session becomes a paper.** Every question in a numbered navigator, click to jump,
   coloured by grade; grading advances to the next *ungraded* question so skipping around
   never strands you; the chapter rail becomes a picker, since the left column now belongs
   to the questions.
3. **Reading typography.** Prompts and worked solutions render through the reader's
   `MarkdownReader` at the reader's prose size, with the shared `ReadingControls` in the
   header — one setting, everywhere text is read.

**Non-goals.** No new item kinds (mcq/order/code stay in their own phases). No change to
the pool's data model, the job, the schedule, or fluency. No re-litigating v0.19.0's
design — this repairs its output, not its shape.

## Exit criteria

1. A regenerated chapter's items each carry the material they act on — judged by reading
   them, which is the criterion v0.19.0 should have had.
2. No stored prompt points outside itself, and none is a passage with nothing asked of it.
3. Every drop is visible in the job receipt; nothing is silently truncated.
4. The navigator jumps, colours by grade, advances to the next ungraded question, and the
   grade still reaches FSRS.
5. Prompt text is reading-sized and adjustable through the shared reading prefs.

## Build

**The self-containment fix caused a second defect, and the second regeneration found it.**
Phrased as "QUOTE IT IN FULL", the instruction made the model emit *bare quotations as
prompts* — three of 35 items were passages with nothing asked of them
(`"membership takes a value in [0, 1]…"` as the entire prompt). A separate miss: the deixis
pattern listed `the|that` and not `this`, so *"Into what logical components does this
quoted sentence break down?"* shipped.

Repaired by putting a floor under the quote requirement rather than weakening it: the
prompt now says the item is a TASK and quoted material *supports* an instruction rather
than replacing it, and `_has_task()` requires a question mark or an imperative verb. Both
failures are counted in the same `dropped` figure — a silent drop would have made the pool
quietly smaller with nothing to read about it.

**A stricter rule was measured and rejected.** Catching bare definite references ("the
claim", "the example") would have caught one more genuine dangler in the live corpus and
deleted two good questions with it — *"What property must a proof system satisfy so that
every formula derivable from a set of premises is true in all models?"* among them. The
narrow pointer rule plus the prompt does the work; the broad rule costs more than it buys.
Recorded here because the temptation to re-add it will come back.

**Navigator behaviour worth keeping.** Reveal and grade are keyed by ITEM, not by position:
jumping back to a question you opened must not hide its solution again. Grading advances to
the next ungraded item and wraps, so a paper worked out of order still finishes.

## Test

- Suite: **159 passed, 29 skipped, 1 xfailed**. Every new test in `test_practice.py` was
  written *from an actual bad item*, after it shipped — the deixis phrases, the bare
  quotations, and the `this` variant are all verbatim from the live pool.
- **Both filter rules were measured against the 35 live items before landing**: together
  they catch exactly the four broken ones and keep the other 31, including the
  quote-plus-construction problems the fix was written to produce ("A formal logic is a
  triple… Using only this information, construct a concrete formal logic for set
  membership").
- Session verified in the running app against the real course: jumped to question 7,
  revealed, graded Good → its square turned green, the counter moved to 1/64, the view
  advanced to question 8, and the `prac-` FSRS row persisted. Prompt type measured at
  16px default, 20px at the top of the reading scale, rendering through
  `.markdown-reader` with no `.concept-md` left in the DOM.
- Chapter 1 regenerated on the deployed stack after each fix, and read each time. The
  receipts are the record: 64 items `{dropped: 10}` → 35 `{dropped: 34}` (the overshoot) →
  **45 items `{authored: 45, dropped: 28, refused: 0}`** — 21 problems, 24 retrieval,
  **0 pointer violations and 0 task-less prompts** across the stored pool.

**Read, not counted.** The same problems that were broken now carry their material:

> Rewrite the following claim in first-order notation using only the predicate B(x) for
> 'x is black', and standard logical symbols: Every raven is black.

> Given the rule *if a card has a vowel on one side it has an even number on the other*,
> and cards showing E, K, 4, 7, list exactly which two cards you would turn over to test
> the rule, and briefly explain why those are the two.

> Using only the ∧-introduction rule, derive (P → Q) ∧ (Q → R) from the premises 'P → Q'
> and 'Q → R'. Show each inference step.

Two worked solutions were hand-checked and are correct (∀x(R(x) → B(x)); the single
∧-introduction step). **One imperfection worth recording rather than hiding:** the raven
prompt says "using only the predicate B(x)" while its solution reasonably introduces R(x)
as well — the prompt is under-specified, not wrong, and the rubric ("the correct predicate
symbols for raven and black") is what the learner grades against. Prompt precision at this
grain is a next-cycle concern, not a blocker.

The drop rate stays high (28 of 73 candidates) and that is the filter doing its job on a
model that still reaches for a pointer roughly a third of the time. It is visible in the
receipt by design; if it ever needs lowering, the prompt is the lever, not the gate.

## Review

**What this cycle actually taught: an exit criterion that tests the mechanism will pass
while the artifact is unusable.** v0.19.0 verified generation, regeneration, inheritance,
session separation and measurement honesty — all live, all against a real book, all
green — and shipped prompts with holes in them. Nothing in that list required anyone to
*read an item and try to answer it*. The waypoint on Item pool always said "that I would
actually sit down and answer"; the cycle's own criteria quietly substituted easier
questions.

**Both rounds of defects were found by using the thing, and neither by a test.** Every
test here was written after the fact, from a real bad item. That is the correct order —
but it means the pool's quality gate is still a person reading output, and the next kinds
(mcq, order, code) will each need their own reading pass rather than inheriting confidence
from this one.

**A fix can be a regression.** The self-containment instruction was correct and made the
output worse, because it was phrased as an absolute and the model obeyed it past the point
of sense. The repair was to put a floor under it, not to retreat from it.
